Where we are

Everything Mobutu built came apart in the decade after 1974, and there are two explanations for it. One is that world prices destroyed him. The other is that his own policies did. Both have evidence behind them, and the mark you earn depends on how you weigh them rather than on which one you pick.

The essay so far

Belgium left the Congo without a trained administration or a settled constitution, so the claim that only strong central control could hold the country together started with real force behind it.

His political policies did produce order, but it was an order built on suppressed opposition and a cult of himself, which strengthened the ruler rather than the state.

The same pattern ran through the economy: nationalisation and Zairianisation genuinely broke foreign ownership, but the assets went to his allies, so a change of owner was not development.

The copper crash made the fall steeper, but it does not explain a state without usable roads and a president whose fortune approached the national debt, so the verdict rests on the policies themselves.

Four sentences. That is a complete line of argument, including the counter argument, and it is more structure than most candidates manage in three pages.
